    About LinguaSpace

    Project Purpose

    LinguaSpace is an academic project exploring linguistic identity, shame, and translanguaging among multilingual students in the Arabian Gulf region, specifically at Carnegie Mellon University Qatar (CMU-Q).

    This platform provides a safe, anonymous space where students can share their experiences with linguistic identity navigation, accent anxiety, code-switching challenges, and the complex negotiations of multilingual life.

    Academic Concepts

    💡 Linguistic Shame (Sara Hillman)

    The feeling of inadequacy or embarrassment about one's language use, accent, or perceived language deficiency. Often experienced by multilingual individuals when navigating between linguistic communities.

    🌐 Translanguaging

    The natural practice of multilingual speakers seamlessly moving between languages in communication. Rather than keeping languages separate, translanguaging recognizes the fluid, dynamic nature of multilingual expression.

    ⏱️ Chronotope (Blommaert)

    The concept that language use is inseparable from specific times and places. Different contexts (classroom vs. home, formal vs. informal) shape which languages and varieties are considered appropriate or valued.

    🎭 Identity Negotiation

    The ongoing process of navigating multiple linguistic identities, especially the feeling of being "not enough" or "too much" in different cultural and linguistic contexts.

    How to Participate

    1. Browse Stories: Explore the six community categories to find experiences that resonate with you
    2. Engage: Upvote stories that speak to you, comment with support and validation
    3. Share: Click "Share Your Story" to contribute your own experiences (all anonymous)
    4. Connect: Find solidarity in shared struggles and celebrate multilingual pride together

    Research Context

    This project builds on research in sociolinguistics, multilingualism, and linguistic anthropology, particularly work by:

    • Sara Hillman on linguistic shame and insecurity
    • Jan Blommaert on sociolinguistic scales and chronotopes
    • Ofelia García on translanguaging pedagogy
    • Studies on Gulf multilingualism and expatriate identity

    Community Guidelines

    🤝 Our Values

    • Validation over Judgment: We support each other's experiences
    • Anonymity & Safety: Share without fear of identification
    • Linguistic Diversity: All languages, accents, and varieties are welcome
    • Empathy First: Assume good faith and respond with kindness

    ✅ Encouraged

    • Sharing personal linguistic experiences and struggles
    • Offering support and validation to others
    • Mixing languages naturally (translanguaging)
    • Asking questions and seeking understanding
    • Celebrating multilingual identity and pride
    • Connecting academic concepts to lived experiences

    ❌ Not Allowed

    • Correcting or policing others' language use
    • Dismissing or minimizing others' experiences
    • Attempting to identify anonymous users
    • Harassment, discrimination, or hate speech
    • Off-topic content unrelated to linguistic identity

    💬 Engaging Meaningfully

    When commenting:

    • Start with validation: "I felt this deeply..." or "You're not alone..."
    • Share similar experiences if relevant
    • Offer support without trying to "fix" the person
    • Avoid giving unsolicited advice
    • Use upvotes generously to show solidarity

    🛡️ Safety & Privacy

    This is a demo application for academic purposes. All data is stored locally in your browser. However:

    • Avoid sharing identifying information
    • Keep stories general enough to maintain anonymity
    • Remember that screenshots can be taken
    • If you need professional support, seek appropriate resources
