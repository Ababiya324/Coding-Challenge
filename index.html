About This Project

Context: Linguistic Identity in the Gulf

At Carnegie Mellon University Qatar, students from over 50 countries navigate a complex multilingual environment. English dominates academic spaces, while Arabic serves as the local lingua franca. Students frequently experience linguistic shame, moments where their accent, language choice, or code-switching is policed or stigmatized.

Theoretical Framework

This project draws on several key concepts:

  • Linguistic Shame (Sara Hillman, 2019): The emotional response to perceived linguistic inadequacy, often resulting from institutional language hierarchies.
  • Translanguaging (García & Wei): The natural, fluid use of multiple languages as a unified repertoire rather than separate systems.
  • Chronotope (Blommaert): How specific times and spaces shape which languages are valued, creating pressure to perform particular linguistic identities.
  • Enoughness: The constant negotiation of being "enough"—Arab enough, Western enough, fluent enough—in institutional spaces that demand impossible authenticity.

Methodology

Stories were collected through anonymous surveys and interviews with CMU-Q students during Fall 2024. All identifying information has been removed to protect participant privacy. The interactive map visualizes the spatial distribution of these experiences across campus, revealing how institutional spaces shape linguistic identity.
